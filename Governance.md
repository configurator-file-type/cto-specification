# Configurator File Type Project Governance Policy

This document is the governance policy for the specifications developed in this
repository. Sections 1 to 5 are the
[Community Specification Governance Policy 1.0](https://github.com/CommunitySpecification/1.0/blob/master/5._Governance.md),
unmodified. Section 6 adds provisions specific to this Project.

The Project is the Configurator File Type Project, a series of Joint Development
Foundation Projects, LLC. It has two Working Groups, the CTO Specification
Working Group and the CIS Specification Working Group. Each Working Group Charter
elects Community Specification mode, which is why Sections 1 to 5 apply. This
repository belongs to the CTO Specification Working Group.

Section 6 is a policy of the Steering Committee under Section 6.7 of the Project
Charter. The order of precedence, highest first, is: the Membership Agreement,
the Project Charter, the Working Group Charter, then this document. **Where this
document conflicts with any of those, they control.** Capitalized terms not
defined here have the meanings in the Project Charter.

This document provides the governance policy for specifications and other documents developed using the Community Specification process in a repository (each a “Working Group”).  Each Working Group and must adhere to the requirements in this document.

## 1.	Roles.

Each Working Group may include the following roles. Additional roles may be adopted and documented by the Working Group.

**1.1.	Maintainer.** “Maintainers” are responsible for organizing activities around developing, maintaining, and updating the specification(s) developed by the Working Group.  Maintainers are also responsible for determining consensus and coordinating appeals.  Each Working Group will designate one or more Maintainer for that Working Group.  A Working Group may select a new or additional Maintainer(s) upon Approval of the Working Group Participants.

**1.2.	Editor.**  “Editors” are responsible for ensuring that the contents of the document accurately reflect the decisions that have been made by the group, and that the specification adheres to formatting and content guidelines. Each Working Group will designate an Editor for that Working Group.  A Working Group may select a new Editor upon Approval of the Working Group Participants.

**1.3.	Participants.**  “Participants” are those that have made Contributions to the Working Group subject to the Community Specification License.

## 2.	Decision Making.

**2.1.	Consensus-Based Decision Making.**  Working Groups make decisions through a consensus process (“Approval” or “Approved”).  While the agreement of all Participants is preferred, it is not required for consensus.  Rather, the Maintainer will determine consensus based on their good faith consideration of a number of factors, including the dominant view of the Working Group Participants and nature of support and objections.  The Maintainer will document evidence of consensus in accordance with these requirements.

**2.2.	Appeal Process.**  Decisions may be appealed be via a pull request or an issue, and that appeal will be considered by the Maintainer in good faith, who will respond in writing within a reasonable time.

## 3.	Ways of Working.

Inspired by [ANSI’s Essential Requirements for Due Process](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/Standards%20Activities/American%20National%20Standards/Procedures,%20Guides,%20and%20Forms/2020_ANSI_Essential_Requirements.pdf), Community Specification Working Groups must adhere to consensus-based due process requirements.  These requirements apply to activities related to the development of consensus for approval, revision, reaffirmation, and withdrawal of Community Specifications.  Due process means that any person (organization, company, government agency, individual, etc.) with a direct and material interest has a right to participate by: a) expressing a position and its basis, b) having that position considered, and c) having the right to appeal. Due process allows for equity and fair play. The following constitute the minimum acceptable due process requirements for the development of consensus.

**3.1.	Openness.**  Participation shall be open to all persons who are directly and materially affected by the activity in question. There shall be no undue financial barriers to participation. Voting membership on the consensus body shall not be conditional upon membership in any organization, nor unreasonably restricted on the basis of technical qualifications or other such requirements.  Membership in a Working Group’s parent organization, if any, may be required.

**3.2.	Lack of Dominance.**  The development process shall not be dominated by any single interest category, individual or organization. Dominance means a position or exercise of dominant authority, leadership, or influence by reason of superior leverage, strength, or representation to the exclusion of fair and equitable consideration of other viewpoints.

**3.3.	Balance.**  The development process should have a balance of interests. Participants from diverse interest categories shall be sought with the objective of achieving balance.

**3.4.	Coordination and Harmonization.**  Good faith efforts shall be made to resolve potential conflicts between and among deliverables developed under this Working Group and existing industry standards.

**3.5.	Consideration of Views and Objections.**  Prompt consideration shall be given to the written views and objections of all Participants.

**3.6.	Written procedures.**  This governance document and other materials documenting the Community Specification development process shall be available to any interested person.

## 4.	Specification Development Process.

**4.1.	Pre-Draft.**  Any Participant may submit a proposed initial draft document as a candidate Draft Specification of that Working Group.  The Maintainer will designate each submission as a “Pre-Draft” document.

**4.2.	Draft.**  Each Pre-Draft document of a Working Group must first be Approved to become a” Draft Specification”.  Once the Working Group approves a document as a Draft Specification, the Draft Specification becomes the basis for all going forward work on that specification.

**4.3.	Working Group Approval.**  Once a Working Group believes it has achieved the objectives for its specification as described in the Scope, it will Approve that Draft Specification and progress it to “Approved Specification” status.

**4.4.	Publication and Submission.**  Upon the designation of a Draft Specification as an Approved Specification, the Maintainer will publish the Approved Specification in a manner agreed upon by the Working Group Participants (i.e., Working Group Participant only location, publicly available location, Working Group maintained website, Working Group member website, etc.).  The publication of an Approved Specification in a publicly accessible manner must include the terms under which the Approved Specification is being made available under.

**4.5.	Submissions to Standards Bodies.**  No Draft Specification or Approved Specification may be submitted to another standards development organization without Working group Approval. Upon reaching Approval, the Maintainer will coordinate the submission of the applicable Draft Specification or Approved Specification to another standards development organization. Working Group Participants that developed that Draft Specification or Approved Specification agree to grant the copyright rights necessary to make those submissions.

## 5. Non-Confidential, Restricted Disclosure.

Information disclosed in connection with any Working Group activity, including but not limited to meetings, Contributions, and submissions, is not confidential, regardless of any markings or statements to the contrary.  Notwithstanding the foregoing, if the Working Group is collaborating via a private repository, the Participants will not make any public disclosures of that information contained in that private repository without the Approval of the Working Group.

## 6. Project-Specific Provisions.

**6.0. Who may participate.** Anyone may make a Contribution to this Working
Group by opening a pull request in this repository. Project membership is not
required. Opening a pull request makes the person who opens it a Participant
(Section 1.3), and is their agreement that the Contribution is made under the
Community Specification License 1.0, limited to the Scope in
[`Scope.md`](Scope.md), or under Apache-2.0 for source code (see
[`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md)), and to this document and the Code of Conduct. A
person who contributes work owned by their employer or another organization
confirms, by opening the pull request, that they are authorized to contribute it
on that organization's behalf **[DECISION]**.

A Member is a party that has executed the Membership Agreement, with its
Affiliates. New Members join on Approval of the Steering Committee, and the
agreement takes effect when the Chairperson countersigns it. A Member Joins a
Working Group by adding its name to that Working Group's list in
[`MEMBERS.md`](MEMBERS.md). Membership is not needed to open an issue or a pull
request, to review one, or to object. Role holders under 6.2 are people from
Members, and votes under 6.2(c) are cast one per Member **[DECISION]**.
Attendance at Working Group meetings by a party that is not a Member follows
Section 3 of the Working Group Charter (Non-Member Agreement) **[DECISION]**.

**6.1. Working Groups and repositories.** Each Working Group develops its
specification in its own repository:

- CTO Specification Working Group: <https://github.com/configurator-file-type/cto-specification>
- CIS Specification Working Group: <https://github.com/configurator-file-type/cis-specification>

Every pull request to this repository is decided by the CTO Specification Working Group.
A change that needs matching changes to both specifications is made as two
linked pull requests, one in each repository, each decided by its own Working
Group and merged together.

Project-wide text is kept identical in both repositories: Sections 1 to 6 of
`Governance.md` (except 6.1 and 6.10, which name this repository's own paths),
`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `LICENSES/`, the Antitrust section of `Notices.md`, and
the Project Members table in `MEMBERS.md`. A change to any of them is made by
a pair of linked pull requests, one in each repository, merged together.

**6.2. Roles and role holders.**

(a) *Who holds which role.* Each Working Group has at least two Maintainers,
employed by different Members, and at least one Editor **[DECISION]**. One person
may hold both roles, and may hold roles in both Working Groups. The current role
holders are recorded in [`MAINTAINERS.md`](MAINTAINERS.md).

(b) *Working Group Lead.* As an additional role under Section 1, each Working
Group names one of its Maintainers as Working Group Lead. The Lead convenes the
Working Group's meetings, sets and circulates agendas, is the Working Group's
point of contact for new Participants and for the Steering Committee, and makes
sure that meeting outcomes are recorded on GitHub under 6.8. The Lead has no
decision-making authority beyond that of any other Maintainer.

(c) *Selection.* Any Working Group Participant may nominate a Participant,
including themselves, for Maintainer, Editor or Working Group Lead. Role holders
are designated by Approval of the Working Group Participants (Sections 1.1 and
1.2). If Consensus cannot be reached, the Working Group Participants vote, with
one vote per Member including its Affiliates, and a simple majority of the votes
cast decides. The outcome is recorded on a GitHub issue and then in
`MAINTAINERS.md` by pull request.

(d) *First designation.* Until a Working Group has a Maintainer, the Chairperson
runs its nominations and determines Consensus under (c), and records the outcome.

(e) *Changes.* A role holder may resign by notice on a GitHub issue. Adding,
replacing or removing a role holder follows (c). Changing a role holder is a
change to `MAINTAINERS.md`, made by pull request and Approved by the affected
Working Group.

**6.3. Steering Committee.** The Steering Committee governs the Project and
approves Final Deliverables (Project Charter 6.1). It decides by Consensus, as
determined by the Chairperson, and failing that by Supermajority Vote: at least
75% of Active Voting Members, one vote per Member including its Affiliates
(Project Charter 6.4, 22). An electronic vote is a yes/no ballot open for at
least 7 days (Project Charter 6.6). Within this repository the Steering Committee
(a) approves changes to the governance files listed in 6.5, (b) receives appeals
of procedural error under Project Charter 6.5, and (c) administers the GitHub
organization, including team membership and branch rules.

**6.4. Classes of change.** Every pull request is one of the following. The
author states the class; a Maintainer may reclassify it.

| Class | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial | Does not change what a conformant file or conformant software must do | Typos, formatting, broken links, clearer wording, an added non-normative example or note |
| Normative | Changes what a conformant file or conformant software must do, or changes a schema | A new or changed field, a changed MUST or SHOULD, a new validation rule, a new file subtype |
| Governance | Changes a file listed in 6.5 | A Scope change, a new Maintainer, a change to this document |

**6.5. Governance files.** `Scope.md`, `Governance.md`, `Notices.md`,
`LICENSE.md`, `LICENSES/`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`,
`MAINTAINERS.md`, `MEMBERS.md` and `.github/CODEOWNERS`. Entries may be added to `Notices.md`
and `MEMBERS.md` as those files describe, without the approvals below.

**6.6. Approvals required to merge.**

| Class | Linked issue | Approving reviews | Minimum time open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial | Not required | One Maintainer or Editor of the Working Group **[DECISION]** | None |
| Normative | Required, labeled `spec-change` | Two, including one Editor of the Working Group, from people employed by at least two different organizations **[DECISION]** | 7 calendar days **[DECISION]** |
| Governance | Required | Steering Committee Approval, recorded on the pull request. No one approves or merges their own pull request. A "Comment" review is not an
approval. An unresolved "Changes requested" review from a Participant blocks
merging until the reviewer withdraws it or a Maintainer records, on the pull
request, a determination of consensus under Section 2.1 that addresses the
objection.

**6.7. Normative changes travel complete.** A normative pull request updates, in
the same pull request, the specification text, the affected schema, at least one
example file, and the changelog. Specification text is drafted prose first and
schema second.

**6.8. Decisions are recorded on GitHub.** Slack, email and meetings are for
coordination. A decision that affects a specification is not made until it is
written on the relevant issue or pull request, with enough of the reasoning that
a Participant who was not present can understand it and object. The person who
merges a pull request confirms this has been done. This implements Sections 2.1,
3.5 and 3.6, and the due process commitment in Section 8 of the Project Charter.
Project Charter Section 14 restricts public disclosure of Project information
that the Project has not itself made public. Adoption of this document is the
Working Groups' standing Approval to publish, on this repository, summaries of
Working Group discussions held in Slack and in meetings. It does not extend to
material a Member has asked to keep out of the public record pending a decision
under Section 14.

**6.9. Contributions enter by pull request only.** Text, schema or examples
proposed in Slack, email, a meeting or a shared document are not Contributions
until the person who authored them, or a co-author who names them, submits them
by pull request from their own GitHub account. No one submits material on behalf
of another person or organization without their permission, and every co-author
is named on the pull request. Each pull request
discloses every known copyright owner of what it contributes (Project Charter
Section 10).

**6.10. Versions and the specification files.** **[DECISION]**

(a) The specification has one living file on `main`,
`spec/cto/specification.md`. Every pull request that changes the specification
edits that file, so that reviewers see exactly
which lines change. There is no separate draft folder: work in progress lives on
the pull request's branch, and `main` is the current Draft Specification (see
Section 4.1 and 4.2).

(b) The specification uses semantic versioning. A version is published in
three parts, in this order:

1. A **release pull request**, opened by a Maintainer, that copies the living
   file unchanged into a new folder named for the full version, for example
   `spec/cto/v0.3.0/cto-specification-v0.3.0.md`; moves that specification's
   `spec/CHANGELOG.md` entries from "Unreleased" to a heading for the new
   version; and updates the README's "latest published version" link. The text
   has already been Approved, so a release pull request needs one Maintainer
   approval, has no minimum open period, and must not change the specification
   text.
2. A **git tag**, `cto-vX.Y.Z`, on the commit that merged the
   release pull request.
3. A **GitHub Release** for that tag, with release notes taken from the
   changelog and the disclaimer in [`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md). A PDF may be
   attached.

(c) A version folder is created once and never changed. Folders are named for the
full version (`v0.3.0/`, `v0.3.1/`), never for a minor version (`v0.3/`), so
that a patch release does not overwrite an earlier one. Tags and Releases are
never moved or edited. A correction to a published version is made in the living
file and published as a new version. The only pull requests that touch
`spec/cto/v*/` are release pull requests, and they only add a
new folder.

(d) The folders that exist from before this policy (`spec/cto/v0.1/`,
`spec/cto/v0.2/`) keep their current names and contents so
that existing links keep working. They are read-only like any other version
folder.

(e) The README shows the latest published version
(linking to its Release and its version folder) and the working draft (linking to
the living file). Text on `main` may include changes that have not yet been
published.
